The blood-brain barrier. Why most drugs can never reach the brain. The brain is guarded by a wall of tightly-joined cells that blocks most molecules from crossing out of the blood. It keeps toxins out — but it also keeps most drugs out. Only small, fat-soluble molecules slip through easily. That is why treating brain conditions is so hard, and why a drug that works everywhere else may do nothing in the brain. The takeaway.
