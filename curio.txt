What caffeine actually blocks. It does not add energy — it hides tiredness. As you stay awake, a molecule called adenosine builds up and makes you sleepy by binding its receptors. Caffeine is shaped just enough like adenosine to sit in those receptors without activating them. So caffeine does not power you up — it blocks the “you are tired” signal. When it wears off, the backed-up adenosine floods in, and the crash hits. The takeaway. Caffeine is a receptor blocker — a tidy example of how shape lets one molecule impersonate another.
