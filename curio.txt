Why ice floats. A quirk of water that keeps lakes alive. Almost every substance gets denser when it freezes. Water is a rebel: its molecules lock into an open, hexagonal lattice that takes up more space, so ice is about 9 percent less dense than liquid water. That is why ice floats — and why lakes freeze top-down, leaving fish a liquid world underneath all winter. The takeaway.
