A spoonful weighing a billion tonnes. The densest thing you can name. When a massive star collapses, its core can be crushed until protons and electrons merge into neutrons packed shoulder to shoulder. The result is a neutron star: a city-sized ball as heavy as the Sun. A single teaspoon of it would weigh about a billion tonnes — matter squeezed to almost unimaginable density. The takeaway. Gravity, pushed to its limit, can crush a whole star into a sphere you could drive across. Here is a question. What is a neutron star made of? A. Ordinary gas. B. Neutrons crushed to extreme density. C. Pure light. D. Liquid iron.
